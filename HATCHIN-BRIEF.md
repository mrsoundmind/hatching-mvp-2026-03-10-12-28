## 2. The Core Idea (expanded)

Think of Hatchin as a **company-in-a-box made of AI colleagues**. When you start a project, you don't get a blank chat window — you get a team. Each member is a "Hatch": a named AI specialist with a real voice, professional judgment, and a domain they own.

The defining qualities that make Hatchin different from a generic AI assistant:

- **They have personalities.** Each Hatch has a distinct voice, communication style, and way of pushing back. Zara the Creative Director will argue with you about your color palette. Alex the PM will disagree when you're wrong about scope.
- **They remember.** Conversations, decisions, project direction, and your working style persist across sessions. Come back tomorrow and your team remembers what you were building and how you like to work.
- **They have expertise.** Thirty real professional roles — product, engineering, design, marketing, data, ops, content — each with deep domain reasoning, not surface-level chat.
- **They collaborate with each other.** One Hatch finishes a spec and hands it off to the right specialist. The engineer writes the architecture, the designer picks it up, the marketer turns it into a launch plan. You watch the handoffs happen.
- **They work autonomously.** Tell a Hatch "go ahead and work on this," and it executes in the background — producing real deliverables (plans, docs, breakdowns, research) — and hands off to teammates. You can wake up to finished work.
- **They self-check.** Risky or important work goes through peer review by another Hatch and safety gates before it's delivered. High-stakes actions wait for your approval.

The emotional promise: **you are no longer building alone.** The product's whole narrative is the shift from "one person doing all of it" to "she leads, they build."

## 4. The Problem Hatchin Solves

**The core problem:** Solo founders, indie makers, and small teams have ideas but not the people to execute them. Hiring a team is expensive and slow. Using AI today means juggling five different single-purpose tools (one for code, one for copy, one for design feedback, one for strategy) — none of which remember each other, your project, or you.

Pain points Hatchin attacks:
- **The loneliness and overload of solo building** — "everything depends on me."
- **Tool sprawl** — "I stopped using five different AI tools. One team, one project, they remember everything."
- **Stateless AI** — every ChatGPT conversation starts from zero; nothing carries over.
- **No real collaboration** — generic AI gives you one perspective; real work needs a PM *and* an engineer *and* a designer who talk to each other.
- **No pushback** — assistants agree with everything; good teammates tell you when you're wrong.
- **Work doesn't get done while you're away** — you have to be in the loop for every keystroke.

## 5. How It Works (the user's journey)

1. **Start a project in under a minute.** No setup, no configuration. Describe your idea or pick a starter pack.
2. **Meet Maya first.** Maya is your "Idea Partner" — the project-level intelligence that greets you, helps you shape the idea, and routes you to the right specialists.
3. **A team auto-hatches.** Relevant Hatches (a PM, engineer, designer, marketer, etc.) appear for your project, organized into teams.
4. **Chat like colleagues.** Real-time chat with streaming responses. Mention a specific Hatch (@engineer) or let the system route your question to whoever's best suited.
5. **Tasks appear automatically.** As you talk, Hatchin detects work that needs doing and proposes tasks — you approve or dismiss.
6. **Deliverables get built.** Hatches generate real artifacts — product specs, pricing briefs, launch plans, content, research — viewable in a side panel with version history, exportable as branded PDFs.
7. **Work happens in the background.** Hand a task to a Hatch and it executes autonomously, hands off to the next specialist, and tells you when it's done — even overnight.
8. **You stay in control.** Risky actions wait for your approval. You can pause, cancel, or redirect at any time.

The promise: **from idea to a working team in under a minute. From a conversation to shipped work while you sleep.**

## 7. Core Features & Capabilities

These are the real, shipped capabilities. Group/name them however the site needs.

**Conversational team chat**
- Real-time, streaming chat with each Hatch — feels like messaging a colleague, not querying a bot.
- Project-level chat (talk to the whole team), team-level chat, and 1-on-1 chats with individual Hatches.
- Mention specific teammates (@designer) or let the system route to the best-suited specialist automatically.
- Human, colleague-style tone — no robotic "Great question!" openers, no bullet-point dumps, genuine reactions and opinions.

**Real personalities with real opinions**
- Every Hatch has a distinct voice, communication style, and domain expertise.
- They push back. They disagree when you're wrong. They have professional standards and critical-thinking patterns specific to their craft.

**Persistent memory**
- The project "brain" — goals, direction, culture, and shared knowledge — persists across every conversation.
- Hatches learn your working style over time and adapt.
- Upload documents (PDF / DOCX / TXT / MD) to the project brain so the team works from your real context.

**Automatic task detection**
- As you chat, Hatchin detects work that needs doing and proposes tasks.
- Lifecycle commands ("mark that done," "make this high priority," "who's on the pricing task?") understood from natural language.
- A task pipeline view shows what's queued, in progress, and shipped.

**Real deliverables**
- Hatches generate actual work products — product specs, pricing briefs, launch plans, research docs, content, and more (15+ deliverable types mapped to the right roles).
- Side-panel artifact viewer with markdown rendering, version history, and "refine this section" iteration.
- Export as a branded, professional PDF with table of contents and attribution.

**Cross-agent handoffs**
- When one Hatch finishes, work routes to the right next specialist automatically.
- Each handoff passes structured context forward — the engineer's spec reaches the designer intact.
- Hatches announce handoffs in character ("Done with the scope, tagging @Engineer").
- You can watch the handoff chain as a visual timeline.

**Autonomous background execution**
- Tell a Hatch to go work on something; it executes in the background via a durable job queue.
- Multi-step work, coordinated across teammates, without you having to drive every step.
- "Work happens while you sleep" — wake up to finished deliverables.
- A daily cost cap protects against runaway spend.

**Self-review, safety, and trust**
- Risky or important work passes through peer review by another Hatch before delivery.
- A three-tier safety system: low-risk auto-completes, mid-risk gets peer-reviewed, high-risk waits for your one-click approval.
- Progressive trust: Hatches earn more autonomy as they prove reliable.
- A "Git-style run tree" shows exactly what each autonomous run did, step by step, with plain-language badges ("✓ Improved" / "⚠ Made worse").

**You're always in control**
- Inline approval cards (Approve / Reject) for high-stakes actions.
- Pause or cancel all autonomous work at any time.
- A 4-level autonomy dial: Observe → Propose → Confirm → Autonomous. You decide how much rope the team gets.
- Live activity feed showing everything the team is doing, with filters and stats (tasks completed, handoffs, cost).

## 9. Target Audience

**Primary persona — The Solo Founder / Indie Maker.**
- One person (or a tiny team) building a product, startup, or side project.
- Has the vision and the drive but lacks the headcount — no PM, no designer, no marketer on payroll.
- Comfortable with software, often technical or semi-technical, but stretched across every discipline.
- Feels the overload ("six tools, forty-seven tabs, everything depends on me") and the isolation of building alone.
- Wants leverage, momentum, and the feeling of having a real team.

**Who they are, concretely (drawn from the testimonial cast):**
- SaaS founders
- Solo developers / indie hackers / "indie makers"
- Bootstrapped founders
- Weekend / side-project builders
- Newsletter and content founders
- Product builders juggling multiple AI tools they want to consolidate

**Secondary audiences:**
- Small startup teams who need to punch above their headcount.
- Non-technical founders who need a team to translate ideas into specs, plans, and assets.
- Creators and operators who want strategy, content, and execution in one place.

**Psychographics / what they value:**
- Speed and momentum over process.
- Autonomy and control (a "brake pedal" — they want to lead, not babysit).
- Honesty and pushback over flattery.
- Tools that respect their time (no setup, no config, free to start).
- The emotional payoff of not being alone.

**Who Hatchin is *not* for (helps sharpen messaging):**
- Large enterprises needing compliance-heavy, multi-tenant deployments (not the current focus).
- People who want a single Q&A assistant and nothing more.
- Teams that already have all the humans they need and just want a code copilot.

## 13. Brand Identity

### Name
**Hatchin** (stylized with a ® on the site: "Hatchin®"). Domain/email uses **hatchin.ai** (e.g., hello@hatchin.ai). The product narrative also personifies **Maya** as the welcoming intelligence.

### Core metaphor
**Hatching.** Ideas are eggs; the team hatches into life around them. "Hatch" = an AI teammate. Signature moments use an **egg-hatching animation** (🥚). Maya's symbol is a four-point sparkle **✦**.

### Voice & Tone
The brand voice is **warm, confident, human, and a little cinematic** — never corporate, never robotic. It speaks to a builder who's tired of going it alone.

Principles (these are enforced in the product's own AI responses too — the brand and the product share a voice):
- Human, colleague tone — not assistant tone.
- No sycophantic openers ("Great!", "Great question!").
- Short, natural sentences. Weave ideas into prose, not bullet dumps. Match the reader's energy.
- Genuine reactions, curiosity, and opinions.
- Emotional honesty: it names the loneliness of solo building and the relief of having a team.
- Confident but not hype-y. Honest about what AI can and can't do ("They're not replacing humans").
- Occasional cinematic, almost literary cadence in hero/manifesto moments ("Ship what you imagine.", "Your team is waiting.").

Words and phrases that are "on brand": *team, teammates, colleagues, build, ship, hatch, idea, remember, lead, alone → not alone, your team is waiting, meet your team.*

Words to avoid: *bot, chatbot (except when contrasting against competitors), assistant (for the Hatches), prompt, query, leverage AI synergy* — and generic SaaS buzzwords.

### Color Palette
The brand runs on a **deep near-black canvas with a cosmic blue-violet glow** and a **warm orange accent** for action.

**Marketing / dark theme (landing + promo):**
- Background (primary): `#0A0C13` (very dark blue-black) — also `#06070A` / `#08090E` in the promo and cards
- Elevated surface: `#0E1018`
- Primary text: `#FFFFFF` / `#F0F0F0`
- Dimmed text: `#A8AAB5`, and translucent whites (`rgba(255,255,255,0.5–0.6)`) for body copy
- Muted text: `#5C5E68`, `#999999`

**Accent / glow (the "cosmic" identity):**
- Accent blue-violet: `#6C82FF`
- Secondary violet: `#9F7BFF`
- Cool blues in the ambient hero gradient: `rgba(44,144,255,…)`, `rgba(106,84,255,…)`, `rgba(35,201,255,…)`
- Success green: `#47DB9A`

**Action / CTA accent (warm orange — the signature pop against the dark):**
- Orange: `#f97316` (primary action), `#fb923c` (hover), `#fdba74`
- Used for buttons, "recommended" pricing tier, FAQ numbering, the `>>>` list markers.

**Per-Hatch colors** exist too (each role has its own hex — blues for product/data, oranges for engineering, purples/pinks for design, greens for marketing, etc.), useful for avatar accents and team visualizations. Maya is teal `#14b8a6`.

### Typography
- **Display / headings:** `Poppins` (semibold, tight tracking) — used for hero and section titles.
- **Accent serif (for emphasis/italic flourishes):** `Instrument Serif` — e.g., the italic *"Ship what you imagine."*
- **Body / UI:** `Inter` (with system-font fallbacks).
- **Monospace "brutalist" accent:** a mono face for spec-sheet/technical numbering (pricing SKUs, FAQ "Q.01", unit labels). Gives a subtle "engineering/blueprint" texture.

### Visual Style
The current site is **premium, dark, cinematic, and "cosmic":**
- Full-screen ambient hero video with a soft gradient blend.
- Animated gradient "aurora" glow and a twinkling star/particle field.
- A subtle film-grain overlay across everything.
- "Liquid glass" rounded buttons (frosted, translucent).
- A **bento grid** for feature highlights.
- A **brutalist spec-sheet** treatment for pricing and FAQ (mono type, unit numbers, "OPERATIONAL" status dots, `SKU:` labels) — a deliberate contrast of cosmic-soft vs. technical-precise.
- Scroll-triggered reveals and a "card rises from dark" section transition (dark → bright → dark).
- A Waitlister-style footer with an animated horizon/aurora and a final big CTA.

Overall mood: **a calm, premium, slightly sci-fi night sky** — the feeling of a team quietly working in the dark while you rest — punched up with one confident warm-orange call to action.

### Logo / wordmark
Currently a clean **"Hatchin" wordmark** in Poppins with a small ® superscript. No icon mark is locked in — an egg, a sparkle (✦), or a hatching motif would all be on-brand if one is needed.

## 18. Things You Might Have Missed (recommendations)

These aren't in the current site but are worth considering for a stronger product story:

1. **Show, don't just tell.** The most compelling parts of Hatchin — watching Hatches hand work off, the live activity feed, the run-tree with "✓ Improved" badges, the artifact panel with version history — are barely visible on the current page. A short product demo (GIF/video/interactive) of an actual handoff or an overnight run would do more than any headline.
2. **Lead with a named character.** "Meet Maya" is a stronger, warmer entry point than "meet your team" abstractly. People bond with characters. Consider letting visitors *talk to Maya* right on the landing page as the hero interaction.
3. **The "while you slept" moment is the killer demo.** "Tell a Hatch at 11:43 PM, wake up to finished work" is the single most differentiated, magical claim. Give it its own beat.
4. **Make the 30 personalities tangible.** A "meet the team" gallery where you can click a Hatch and hear their voice/see how they push back would sell the "real personalities" promise far better than a number.
5. **Name the enemy clearly.** The "five disconnected AI tools" pain and "ChatGPT is a chatbot, Hatchin is a team" contrast are your sharpest hooks — they deserve prominent placement, not just an FAQ line.
6. **Be explicit about control.** Founders fear runaway autonomous AI. The autonomy dial (Observe → Propose → Confirm → Autonomous), the approval cards, and "pause anytime" are reassurance features — surface them as a benefit ("you lead, always"), not buried settings.
7. **Pick one primary persona and write to them.** The copy currently casts a wide net (founders, indie hackers, newsletter writers, weekend builders). Solo/indie founders are the clear center of gravity — leaning fully into that one person's story will convert better than trying to speak to everyone.
8. **Address the trust/honesty question head-on.** "Can AI really replace a team?" — the honest "no, it's the team you don't have yet" answer is disarming and credible. Don't hide it in the FAQ; it's a strength.
